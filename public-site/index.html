What is a Flow?

A Flow is a habit or practice with a schedule built in. A workout routine, a meal plan, a reading plan—or something entirely your own. Create one, find one in the feed, or share a Flow with a friend and try it together.

Instead of saving a post you never come back to, save a Flow that returns in rhythm to push you forward.

What happens when I save a Flow?

Saving keeps a Flow for whenever you’re ready. Open it, look through the activities and dates, then carry it into your calendar.

That’s where calendar meets social: a practice you discovered in the feed becomes part of your day. Saving alone doesn’t add events; carrying is the step that brings the practice into your time.

Can we do Flows together?

Yes. Invite people when you create a Flow, or choose Together on a Flow that is open to requests. Once the invitation or request is accepted, you have a shared place to practice and talk about it.

Want a book club? The Reading House lets you invite readers and give each sitting a date. Keep the house for invited readers, or let others discover it in the Commons.

Can I create my own Flow?

Yes. Give it a name, add the activities, and decide when they happen. Flow Studio lets you plan individual dates or repeating days, so you can build a practice around your life.

Keep it for yourself, invite people to do it with you, send it to a friend, or post it to your profile so others can discover it. Your idea could become someone else’s next habit.

Do I have to post or share anything?

No. You can explore the feed, carry Flows, and make time for yourself without publishing a post.

Your Journal stays private. If you want to share a reflection, you choose the words and preview the post first. Shared reflections appear on your profile and in the community feed for signed-in members. Keeping it to yourself is always an option.

What is a decan, and why ten days?

A decan is ten days in the Kemetic calendar. In ḥꜣw, each decan has its own theme and purpose: an invitation to notice something different, try a practice, and see what stays with you.

As it closes, “These ten days” gathers the moments you recorded and gives you room to reflect. The ten-day review works alongside each Flow’s own schedule.

Do I need to know the Kemetic calendar?

No. Start with the dates you know. Tap ḥꜣw at the top of the calendar to switch between Gregorian and Kemetic views.

When a month or decan catches your interest, tap its name to explore the story and meaning behind it. You can learn as you go, one day at a time.

What if I miss a few days?

Come back when you can. You don’t need a perfect record to reflect. “These ten days” works with the moments you recorded, and you can add something you remember—even when the rest of the review is empty.

Write a few words, keep them in your Journal, or leave the question open. There is room for the days that didn’t go to plan, too.

Where should I start?

What would you like more time for? Tell ḥꜣw when you begin, and it will suggest a Ma’at Flow for you—whether you want to read more, nourish yourself, reconnect with the sky, find steadiness, or make room for imagination.

Take a look through the practice, then carry it when you’re ready. Its next activity will be waiting in your Day View. You can also head straight to the feed and see what catches your interest.
